PX-42F-Plus User Manual

The screen, its fonts, the settings menu, charging and firmware updates

The PX-42F-Plus runs Plus42, an enhanced HP-42S, on the PX-42F calculator. This manual covers what the calculator adds around Plus42: how the screen is laid out, which font is used when, every option in the settings menu, and the commands it adds. For the calculator's functions themselves (the stack, programming, the solver, matrices, units, equations), see the Plus42 and HP-42S documentation.

1. Special keys

These key combinations belong to the PX-42F-Plus itself. They work on the calculator screen, and the last three in the settings menu too.

KeysWhat they do
ON (the EXIT key)Turns the calculator on. Its memory comes back exactly as it was when it was turned off.
SHIFT then EXITOpens the settings menu. On the HP-42S this is OFF; here, OFF is the first item of the menu. Pressing ENTER, SHIFT EXIT or EXIT turns the calculator off. ENTER also works because the Off option is selected.
Hold EXIT for 2 secondsTurns the calculator off.
Hold SHIFT and − together for ½ secondTakes a screenshot of the screen as it was when SHIFT went down. A box asks at once what to do with it: SAVE, the default (ENTER or F1), saves it to /SCREENSHOTS on the drive as a 250 × 122 PNG named by date and time (a screenshot can be used as a splash image); CANCEL (EXIT, ← or F6) drops it. With a printer that prints on paper chosen in Printer (HP 82240B or USB ESC/POS), the box also has PRINT (an arrow, then ENTER), which prints it in landscape, turned a quarter, whatever Screen Print says (any key stops it).

The calculator also turns itself off after the Sleep Timer, and when the battery runs empty (about 3.45 V).

Typing in ALPHA

While you type text in ALPHA, the keyboard has three modes, as on the DM42. The status bar shows which one is on: [ ], [a] or [A]. Press ALPHA (SHIFT then ENTER) while in ALPHA for the next mode. To leave ALPHA, press EXIT. The calculator remembers the last mode, also after it is turned off, and the next ALPHA starts in it. The first time, ALPHA starts in [ ].

ModeWhat the keys do
[ ] HP-42SEvery key works as on the HP-42S. Letters come from the ALPHA menu on the top row. STO and RCL are ASTO and ARCL, and R/S runs. Use this mode for numeric local labels such as LBL 01.
[a] and [A] lettersEach key types the letter printed on it: lowercase in [a], capitals in [A]. SHIFT and STO or RCL is ASTO or ARCL: for a register number, type its first digit with SHIFT and the rest without (ASTO 01 is SHIFT 0 1); for a name, type the letters. SHIFT and I J K L M N O types π ( ) " < > ^. SHIFT and any other letter key types the same letter. SHIFT and a digit key types the digit. SHIFT and ÷ × − + types ÷ × − +. R/S types ?, also with SHIFT (to run a program, leave ALPHA first). The dot key types a comma, as on the HP-41C, and SHIFT and the dot key types the period. The menu on the bottom line is hidden while you type. Press SHIFT to show it, then a top-row key opens a group of letters, punctuation or symbols. ↑ ↓ change the menu's page, with or without SHIFT; its second page stays on the screen. Once a menu is on the screen, the top-row keys work it without SHIFT (in [a] they type lowercase, in [A] capitals). After you pick an item the menu goes away.

In program mode, hold SHIFT down and press ↑ or ↓ for BST or SST while typing text. Hold ↑ down to keep stepping back.

2. The screen

The screen is 250 × 122 pixels. From top to bottom it has three parts:

  1. The status bar: a black bar 12 pixels tall. See The status bar.
  2. The lines: the stack, messages, programs, the equation editor, whatever Plus42 is showing.
  3. The menu: six tabs along the bottom, one for each of the six top-row keys, whenever a menu is open.

The lines

Plus42 decides what each line holds; the PX-42F-Plus decides how big they are drawn.

  • The space below the status bar is shared equally between the lines, and each line sits in the middle of its share. So there is always space above the first line and below the last one.
  • The stack's labels are drawn as X: Y: Z: T:, plus L: for LASTx and A: for ALPHA, when those are shown on top (LTOP, ATOP).
  • In the large font, and in the smaller ones when there is room, a dotted line separates every two lines. It is either between all of them or none, never in some gaps only.
  • A plot fills the whole area below the status bar, drawn from Plus42's own pixels at the same scale across and down.

The menu

A menu always looks the same, whatever the number of lines: six black tabs, 14 pixels tall, labelled in the small font. Each tab belongs to the key below it.

  • A label is cut short at five characters.
  • An item that is the current setting (for example RAD in the MODES menu while in RAD mode) is underlined.
  • When a menu is open, the lines share the space above the tabs. When no menu is open, the bottom row is one more line of text and the lines share the whole area.

TOP.FCN (SHIFT 0) opens with a row of Plus42 features that have no key of their own, DIRS UNITS R↑ X<> VIEW EQN; ↓ or ↑ switch to the HP-42S's Σ+ 1/X √X LOG LN XEQ and back. DIRS opens the directories, UNITS the units catalog, EQN the equation list. X<> and VIEW ask for a register, as on the HP-42S. With SHIFT the row is DIR.FCN UNIT.FCN PLOT %CH SST↑ EQN.FCN: the directory, unit and equation function menus, PLOT, percent change, and SST↑ (step out of a subroutine). In the equation editor, TOP.FCN keeps its one row of functions.

3. The status bar

The status bar has two halves: information on the left, and the calculator's indicators on the right.

{ HOME }▶↑↓↳°
TUE-OCT-21 11:42AM▶↑↓↳°

Both bars show every indicator at once, only to show where each one appears. Normally only the ones that apply are lit.

Left: the directory, or the date and time

Plus42's HEADER setting (DISP menu, third page, HDR) chooses what the left side shows:

  • HEADER on (after Memory Clear): the current directory, such as { HOME } or { HOME MATH }. After it come any extras that are switched on:
    • HFLG (HFLAGS): the numbers of any of flags 0–10 that are set, for example 13 for flags 1 and 3.
    • HPOL (HPOLAR): a ∡ while the calculator is in POLAR mode.
    • In the BASE menus, the base and word size, such as HEX 36 SW. In TVM, its BEGIN/END status.
    There is room for 22 characters. A longer path loses its beginning, shown as …, so the directory you're in always stays in view.
  • HEADER off: the date and time, in the formats set in Date & Time, for example TUE-OCT-21 11:42AM or 2026-10-21 11:42.

Right: the indicators

From the right edge leftwards. These are the icons as the calculator draws them, enlarged; only the ones that apply are shown.

IconMeaning
Battery: four bars when full, none when nearly empty. Always shown.
DEG: angles in degrees. One of the three angle icons is always shown.
RAD: angles in radians.
GRAD: angles in grads.
Shift: SHIFT is active; the next key does its shifted function.
More menu rows: the open menu has more rows; ↑ and ↓ page through them.
Running: a program is running.
Alarms: at least one alarm is set. See Alarms.
Printing: the calculator is sending to the printer. It's the print annunciator of Plus42 and the HP-42S. With the HP 82240B, it goes off when the last line has been sent, so the printer may still be finishing that line. See Printer.
ALPHA keyboard: shown while you type text in ALPHA. [ ] is the HP-42S's keys, [a] types lowercase letters, [A] capitals. See Typing in ALPHA.

The angle icons are the HP-42S's own characters °, π and G. The ∡ sign is left to Plus42's HPOLAR mark on the left side, where it means POLAR mode.

4. Lines and fonts

The number of rows is Plus42's own setting, and the font follows from it: the PX-42F-Plus always uses the biggest font that fits. The row count includes the menu's row (with no menu open, that row is a line of text).

After Memory Clear the calculator starts with 5 rows and ATOP on: ALPHA, T, Z, Y and X in the large font, or ALPHA, Z, Y and X above a menu.

Changing the number of rows

Press SHIFT DISP (the E key) and page down to the third page:

Other settings on the DISP menu's later pages change what the lines show:

  • HDR, HFLG, HPOL (HEADER, HFLAGS, HPOLAR): the left side of the status bar. See The status bar.
  • LTOP: a line for LASTx (L:) at the top.
  • ATOP: a line for ALPHA (A:) at the top.
  • 1LINE / NLINE: whether a long number in X stays on one line or wraps onto more.

5. The three fonts

The calculator screen uses three fonts. Each is either the built-in one or a font of your own from the drive, chosen in Settings → Fonts.

The built-in fonts

Each map shows the 128 characters in a 16 × 8 grid. A character's code is its row (00 to 70, in hexadecimal) plus its column (0 to F).

Making your own font

A font is a PNG image, called a font sheet, with the 128 characters in a grid of 16 across and 8 down, in code order: the character with code c is in row c ÷ 16, column c mod 16. Dark pixels are ink. The sizes are fixed:

FontFolderImage sizeCells
Large/FONTS/LARGE/175 × 13510 × 16, with 1 pixel between cells
Small/FONTS/SMALL/128 × 968 × 12, no gaps
Mini/FONTS/MINI/96 × 646 × 8, no gaps

The easiest way to start is from the built-in font's own sheet. It's on the drive, in /FONTS/BUILTIN/: LARGE.png, SMALL.png and MINI.png. Copy it to the computer and edit the copy. The originals are read-only, and the calculator writes them again if they go missing or change. If your computer keeps the copy read-only too (locked, on a Mac), clear that in the file's Get Info or Properties. Copy the finished PNG into its folder with USB Drive, then choose it in Settings → Fonts.

The settings menu, the boot screen and the clock on the splash screen always use the built-in fonts. That way a font that turns out unreadable can always be undone.

6. The settings menu

Press SHIFT then EXIT to open it. It opens on the home screen: up to three rows of seven icons. The bar at the top names the picked icon and shows the date and time. The tabs at the bottom say what the top row of keys does. At first the top row is Off, USB Drive, Plus42, Alarms, Apps, Games and Settings; the second row is empty, for your favourite apps and games (see Apps and games); Help and Info are at the right of the third row. Off is always first; the others can be moved (below).

Moving around

  • On the icons, the top row of keys (Σ+ to XEQ) works the tabs under them: ENTER, the arrows ← ▲ ▼ →, and EXIT. ← → go to the icon before and after, as 4 6 do; ▲ ▼ a row up and down, as 8 2 do. With SHIFT on, the tabs on the home screen and in Apps and Games are MOVE (below), FAV or UNFAV (only where it applies; see Apps and games) and BACK, which only puts the plain tabs back; on an icon with neither, SHIFT changes nothing. In Apps and Games the last plain tab is BACK, to the home screen. The keypad's ↑ ↓ step to the icon before and after. 5 opens, like ENTER. The pages inside (Settings, Plus42, USB Drive) have the same tabs, and nothing under SHIFT.
  • Past the end of a row, right goes on at the start of the next one, and left at the end of the one before. Past the bottom, down goes on at the top, and up past the top at the bottom.
  • The picked icon is drawn grey (its black is dithered) inside a thin frame with rounded corners. Its name is in the bar at the top. With more than three rows, the screen scrolls, and a bar at the right edge shows where you are.
  • In a list, ↑ ↓ move (and 8 2). Items show their current value at the right.
  • The tabs along the bottom of every screen are for the top row of keys, as on the icons. A screen shows only the tabs that do something there: the first names what ENTER does (ENTER, SAVE, USE...), ↑ ↓ on the fourth and fifth move, when there's more than one row, and EXIT on the sixth goes back or leaves without a change, as the EXIT key does. Each tab always has the same place.
  • Where you choose one of several (Time Format, Date Format, Menu Style...), each has a round button, and the one in use is filled. ENTER (or its tab) chooses. Settings that are on or off have a check box; ENTER ticks it.
  • In a form (a new alarm, say), ENTER changes the picked field. A choice opens a list under it: ↑ ↓ pick, ENTER chooses, EXIT closes it.
  • ENTER opens the picked icon or item.
  • EXIT or ← goes back one level. On the home screen, ← returns to the calculator.
  • EXIT on Off, or SHIFT then EXIT anywhere, turns the calculator off.
  • A number: type it, or step it with ↑ ↓; ENTER accepts, EXIT cancels.
  • A time: the hours, minutes (and seconds) side by side, the picked one highlighted. Type two digits and it moves on to the next, or step it with ↑ ↓; the ← → tabs pick another. With the 12-hour clock, AM/PM is the last one: ↑ ↓ switch it.
  • A date: a month's calendar. 4 6 move a day, ↑ ↓ a week, and the tabs a month or a year.
  • A name or text: typed with the letters on the keys, and SHIFT then a digit key for a digit. ← deletes the last character, ENTER accepts, EXIT cancels. This screen has no tabs, because the top row of keys types A to F.
  • The menu turns the calculator off after the Sleep Timer, just as the calculator screen does.

The settings are saved when you change them, and are kept when the calculator is turned off, reset or reflashed.

Moving icons

  1. Pick the icon and press SHIFT then MOVE (the first key of the top row). The bar says Move: and the icon gets a double frame.
  2. Carry it with ↑ ↓, 4 6 8 2 or the ▲ ▼ tabs. It passes over the other icons; they stay where they are.
  3. Press DONE, ENTER or 5 to put it down. CANCL, EXIT or ← puts it back where it was.

The place the icon came from is left empty. Put it down in an empty place and nothing else moves; put it down on another icon and the two change places. While an icon is carried, a row of empty places opens after the last icon, so you can leave gaps to group your icons. Picking an icon (not carrying one) skips the empty places.

Off can't be moved, and nothing goes before it. The icons in the Apps and Games folders move the same way; their places are kept in ORDER.TXT in the folder (/BIN/APPS, /BIN/GAMES), and an app added to the drive goes into the folder's first empty place. The home screen's places, blanks included, and the favourites are kept in /HOME.TXT on the drive, by name, so they stay when the calculator is reset or reflashed. A new favourite goes into the first empty place. Delete /HOME.TXT to go back to the first order with no favourites.

Help

A short guide to the menu: the tabs and keys of the icons, what SHIFT shows, moving icons, favourites, lists and forms, and turning off. It's longer than the screen: ▲ ▼ (the arrow keys or the tabs) turn the pages, SHIFT then an arrow goes to the start or the end, and the page shows at the top right. Info works the same way.

Off

Turns the calculator off. ENTER or EXIT on this icon does it.

Settings

The gear opens a page of eight icons: Date & Time, Fonts, Sounds, Printer, Splash Image, Sleep Timer, Menu Style and Reset, each described below. It works like the home screen; its icons don't move.

Reset

Clears Plus42's memory and restarts the calculator, after asking in an alert box (OK, or CANCEL, EXIT or ← to leave it): programs, variables, the stack and Plus42's own modes are gone, as after a Memory Clear. Alarms, the stopwatch, the settings and the files on the drive stay. The RESET button on the back does the same but also clears the alarms and the stopwatch. In the List style, Reset is the last line.

Menu Style

Icons, the pages of icons described here, or List: the whole menu as one list of text, as in earlier firmware. Off, Apps, Games, Date & Time, Alarms, Sounds, Fonts, Sleep Timer, Splash Image, Printer, States, Programs, USB Drive, Menu Style, Info and Reset; Apps and Games each open a list of their programs. ↑ ↓ move, ENTER opens, EXIT goes back. The menu changes at once, and the choice is kept with the other settings. In the List style, Menu Style is near the end of the list.

Plus42

The calculator icon opens a page for Plus42's memory: States and Programs.

Apps and games

Programs written for the PX-42F itself: games and apps. Each is a .bin file on the drive, in /BIN/APPS or /BIN/GAMES, and has its icon in the Apps or the Games folder on the home screen, in name order at first; MOVE moves them as on the home screen. ENTER on its icon starts it. To add one, copy its .bin into either folder with USB Drive. To remove one, delete the file. None come with the calculator.

Favourites. In a folder, SHIFT then FAV (the second key of the top row) moves the picked app or game to the home screen, into its first empty place, and out of its folder, leaving its place there empty. On the home screen, SHIFT on a favourite shows UNFAV on the second key, which sends it back to its folder, into the first empty place there. Its place on the home screen is left blank, so the other icons stay where they are. A favourite can be moved like the other icons.

The icon comes inside the .bin file. A game or app without one gets a frame with the first letter of its name. Its name is in the bar at the top when it's picked.

Only install apps you trust. A game or app is a program that runs on the calculator with full access: it can read, change and delete any file on the drive, including your saved states and programs. The calculator checks that the file isn't damaged, not that it is safe. Keep a copy of your states (USB Drive) before trying an app from somewhere you don't know.

  • Leaving. EXIT leaves a game or app, back to the icons it was started from, after asking: a box says "Quit SNAKE?" with QUIT and STAY. ENTER quits; EXIT or ← stays, and a game carries on where it was. Some apps use EXIT to go back a screen, and ask when it would leave them. Held for 2 seconds, EXIT turns the calculator off without asking. The Sleep Timer turns it off too.
  • Your memory is safe. Before a game starts, the calculator saves its memory to the drive, and takes it back afterwards. If a game crashes or hangs, the calculator restarts with its memory as it was, and the start screen says where it stopped.
  • Their own files. A game keeps its files (a best score, settings) in a folder named after it, next to it: /BIN/GAMES/SNAKE/ for SNAKE.bin.

If one can't start, the calculator says why:

MessageMeaning
Not a PX-42F appThe file isn't a program for the PX-42F.
Needs a newer firmwareIt was made for a later firmware. Update the firmware.
The file is damagedIts checksum is wrong: copy it again.
Too big (128 KB at most)It needs more memory than a game or app gets.
Can't save the memoryThe drive is full or can't be written, so the calculator won't risk your memory.

Making your own. Games and apps are written in C with the PX-42F App Template, a separate project. It covers the screen and fonts, the keys, the clock, sounds, files on the drive, the USB console, the infrared LED and the battery, and it includes a simulator that runs a game on a computer. Its README explains how to start. The Snake game is built on it. A game's icon is a file icon.txt in its project: 32 lines of 32 characters, # for black.

Date & Time

ItemWhat it does
Set TimeStarts at the time now. Change the hours and minutes, then press ENTER when the seconds reach :00: the seconds start from 0 then.
Time Format24-Hour or 12-Hour.

The formats are the calculator's own: Plus42's DMY, MDY, YMD, CLK12 and CLK24 commands change them too, and the setting follows. DOW-MON-DAY is a PX-42F-Plus format; to Plus42 it counts as MDY. The status bar (with HEADER off), the settings menu's title bar and the splash screen's clock use these formats.

Time Calibration

  • Correction: choose Clock Runs Slow, Clock Runs Fast or No Correction, then how many tenths of a second a day it loses or gains (15 is 1.5 seconds).
  • Measured: lets the calculator measure its own drift. Set the exact time to the second (hours, minutes, seconds) and press ENTER exactly at that second. Do it again after a day or more, and at least 6 hours later: the calculator then shows how many seconds a day it gained or lost, and offers to apply that as the correction. Once something is measured, Measured offers Apply or Enter Exact Time. Setting the time exactly from a computer counts too.

The clock keeps running while the calculator is off, but a full reset (the RESET button, or a flat battery) loses the time.

Alarms

Every alarm that is set, earliest first, with a date, a time and the text. A ! in front means the alarm is past due. ↑ and ↓ pick one. The tabs along the bottom are for the top row of keys, and only those that do something show: with no alarms there is only NEW.

  • NEW sets a new alarm.
  • EDIT (or ENTER) changes the picked alarm. An alarm an app set can only be deleted: the app manages its own.
  • DEL deletes the picked alarm, after asking.
  • ↑ and ↓ pick, as the arrow keys do.

Programs set alarms with XYZALM: see Alarm commands.

A new or edited alarm has the same fields as XYZALM, and a sound. ENTER changes the picked field. Time and Date open the time picker and the calendar; the others open a list under the field, where ↑ ↓ pick, ENTER chooses and EXIT closes it. The tabs are SAVE, ↑, ↓ and EXIT (leaves without a change).

  • Time: hours, minutes and seconds, then AM or PM with the 12-hour clock. A new alarm starts at the next whole hour.
  • Date: in the Date Format's order. A new alarm starts today, or tomorrow if the next whole hour is after midnight.
  • Repeat: None, every hour, day or week, or Other...: any time from one second to 9999 hours, in hours, minutes and seconds.
  • Kind: Message shows the message and plays the sound. Run program runs the program, always (↑↑LABEL in XYZALM). Run when off runs it if the calculator is off or showing the clock, and otherwise acts as a message alarm (↑LABEL).
  • Message or Program: Type..., then the messages of the other alarms, or the calculator's programs, by their global labels. Typing uses the letters on the keys, and SHIFT then a digit key for a digit. A message can be up to 24 characters, with spaces and : , ? _. A program's label can be up to 7 letters and digits.
  • Sound: Plus42 tones (the default), a WAV file from /SOUNDS or a song from /MIDI. The one you choose plays, and SAVE or EXIT stops it. The file names are listed with their .wav or .mid ending, up to 64 of them, and only files directly in those folders. A Run program alarm only runs its program, so it has no sound.

SAVE sets the alarm. A time that has already passed isn't taken: change the time or the date.

When an alarm comes due, a short sound plays again once a second after the first few seconds, as the tones do. A sound longer than the alarm's 20 seconds plays to its end. Any key stops it, along with the alarm. An alarm that repeats keeps its sound. If the file has gone from the drive when the alarm comes due, you hear the Plus42 tones instead. Clearing flag 26 silences all of them. The two tones that remind you of past-due alarms at power on, or that warn of an alarm set for a time already past, don't change.

  • A set (built in: Typewriter): three sounds, chosen by what the key does. Choosing a set plays all three in order.

Your own key sounds. Copy WAV files into /SOUNDS/KEY on the drive:

  • A single sound: /SOUNDS/KEY/<name>.wav, at most 250 ms.
  • A set: a folder /SOUNDS/KEY/<name>/ with ENTRY.wav, OP.wav and ENTER.wav, at most 100 ms each. A missing OP.wav sounds like ENTRY.wav, and a missing ENTER.wav like OP.wav.

Longer sounds are cut short with a fade. Short sounds (30–60 ms) suit fast typing best, because each key press cuts off the sound before it.

Other sounds

All the calculator's sounds are WAV files in /SOUNDS on the drive. Replace any of them with your own:

A deleted file comes back as the built-in one at the next start. A built-in sound you haven't replaced is updated by itself when a new firmware changes it. Sounds you've replaced are never touched.

Clock commands from the HP-41 Time Module

These work as on an HP-41CX, with the same XROM codes. Pick them from the Catalog's PX42 page, under CLOCK, ALARM and SW, or type them with XEQ ALPHA, from the keyboard or in a program.

  • CLOCK shows the running clock on the top line. The next key clears it and does its usual job. In a program, CLOCK also stops the program, and R/S goes on. The clock stays up until the Sleep Timer turns the calculator off.
  • CLKT makes CLOCK show the time, updated every second: 3:15:07 PM. CLKTD makes it show the time and date, updated every minute, which saves power: 3:15 PM 01/21. CLK12, CLK24 and the date format commands set the rest of the format.
  • T+X moves the clock by X, in the form HHHH.MMSSss, forward or back with its sign, into another day if needed. Use it for time zones and daylight saving. It doesn't affect the clock's correction.
  • CORRECT sets the time from X, as HH.MMSSss, and corrects the clock's rate from how far it had drifted since it was last set exactly. It only corrects the rate if that was at least 6 hours ago and the error is under 30 minutes. Otherwise it only sets the time. A longer gap gives a better result, because a key press is only good to about 0.1 s. The HP-41CX manual advises at least 30 hours. This is the same correction as Settings → Date & Time → Time Calibration.
  • SETAF sets the accuracy factor from X, and RCLAF recalls it. This is the HP-41's own measure of the correction, and it isn't in seconds per day: one pulse of 1/10240 s is added every X seconds, or taken away if X is negative. 0.1 gains 84.4 s/day, and 99.9 gains 0.08 s/day. 0 clears it, as does anything from 99.95 up. The calculator keeps the correction in tenths of a second per day, so RCLAF can give a different number from the one you set, mostly for large factors: 50 comes back as 42.2.

Alarm commands from the HP-41 Time Module

These use the HP-41CX's XROM codes too. You can also set an alarm without a program, in Settings → Alarms → New Alarm. To set an alarm, put the repeat in Z, the date in Y, the time in X and the text in ALPHA, then run XYZALM:

  • X, the time: HH.MMSSs, 24-hour. −1 to −11 also mean 1 to 11 p.m.
  • Y, the date: in the current date format, up to the year 2199, or 0 for today.
  • Z, the repeat: HHHH.MMSSs, from 1 second up to 10,000 hours, or 0 for no repeat.
  • ALPHA, what it does: see the table. ↑ is on the ALPHA menu's second page, under the arrows (ARROW). In the [a] and [A] keyboards, SHIFT and the key for ^ also types it.

Playing sounds from a program

Four commands the PX-42F adds to Plus42. Type them with XEQ ALPHA, from the keyboard or in a program. They aren't in the catalog. For the two that play, the name of the file goes in ALPHA first:

  • PLAYWAV starts /SOUNDS/name.wav from the drive. A / in the name is a folder: "TONES/TONE3".
  • PLAYMID starts the song /MIDI/name.mid.
  • SNDWAIT waits until the sound playing has ended. With nothing playing it does nothing.
  • STOPSND stops the sound playing.

Both go on at once, and the sound keeps playing while the calculator or the program carries on, even after the program ends. The speaker plays one sound at a time, so a new sound replaces the one playing. To hear one sound after another, put SNDWAIT between them:

"BOOT"
PLAYWAV
SNDWAIT
"Canon"
PLAYMID

R/S stops the sound. If a program is running, it stops too; if not, the press does nothing else. While you're typing in ALPHA, typing a command or in program mode, R/S does its usual job, and so it does for a program waiting in GETKEY. EXIT stops the sound only during SNDWAIT, and turning the calculator off always stops it. Key presses don't interrupt the sound, and they make no key sound while it plays. Clearing flag 26 silences PLAYWAV and PLAYMID, as it does TONE and BEEP.

Nonexistent means the file isn't there, or a WAV that can't be played. Alpha Data Is Invalid means ALPHA is empty or holds something that can't be a file name. Invalid Data means a song the synthesizer can't play, and Insufficient Memory a song over 128 KB or too big for the memory left.

Fonts

ENTER on a font pulls down its list. Choosing another one shows all 128 of its characters: press ENTER (USE) to use it, or EXIT to keep the one you had. A sheet of the wrong size is refused, with the size it should be. See The three fonts.

Sleep Timer

Turns the calculator off after this many minutes without a key press: 0 to 999, where 0 means never. The default is 10 minutes, the HP-42S's own. The timer doesn't run while a program runs, while Plus42's "continuous on" (flag 44) is set, or while a computer is connected over USB.

Splash Image

The picture the screen shows while the calculator is off, with the date and time across the top, updated every minute.

  • Timer: how many minutes the picture stays up after turning off, 0 to 999. After that the screen goes blank. With 0, it's blank straight away. The default is 10.
  • Image: Built-In, or a PNG from /SPLASH on the drive. ENTER pulls down the list; choosing another image shows it full screen, as it will look. Press ENTER (USE) to use it, or EXIT to keep the one you had. The best size is 250 × 122 pixels, and the PNG must not be interlaced. A screenshot works as is.

Showing the picture costs a little battery while off: about 200 µA against 135 µA blank.

Printer

Picking a printer asks first, in a box that says what it means: OK keeps it, CANCEL (or EXIT) keeps the one before. Off asks nothing.

  • Off: printing commands do nothing (Plus42's PROFF).
  • USB Serial: printing goes to a second USB serial port named PX-42F Printer. Open it in a terminal program on the computer to see the output. Choosing it also turns Plus42's printer on (PRON).
  • HP 82240B: printing goes by infrared to an HP 82240B printer, as on the HP-42S. It works only with the HP 82240B, not with other infrared printers. Aim the calculator at the printer's window. Text prints in the printer's own characters. The characters it lacks (√, Σ, π, the arrows and others) print as graphics in Plus42's shapes. With it, a second row, Screen Print, pulls down how PRLCD prints the screen:
    • Portrait (the first time): the screen redrawn to fit the paper, in Plus42's own small characters: the status bar's text, the lines with the dotted lines between them, and the menu. It takes about 20 seconds. The layouts of 6 rows and more are too wide for the paper; they print in landscape.
    • Landscape: the screen dot for dot, with the PX's fonts. The screen is wider than the paper, so it prints turned a quarter: turn the paper a quarter clockwise to read it. It takes about 2 minutes.
    EXIT there keeps the one chosen before. The printer can't tell the calculator when it is ready. So the calculator waits as long as the slowest printer on nearly flat batteries needs, 1.8 seconds a line. DELAY (in PRINT, as on the HP-42S) changes that: 0 to 1.9 seconds a line from X, kept through OFF, back to 1.8 at Memory Clear. A printer on fresh batteries or its adapter keeps up with less; too little and it loses lines. A long listing (PRP, PRV, PRUSR) takes that long, with ▶ and the printer icon in the status bar. EXIT or R/S stops it, and other keys wait until it ends. They stop PRLCD too, and a program printing with it, but it takes up to 2 seconds, because a line that has begun is always sent whole, or the printer would misread what follows. The printer also finishes what it already has, so the paper can go on for a little. Choosing it also turns Plus42's printer on (PRON).
  • USB ESC/POS: printing goes to a small 58 mm thermal receipt printer (one that takes ESC/POS commands) on the calculator's USB-C port, by a USB-C to USB-C cable (or an OTG adapter and the printer's own cable). The printer runs on its own battery; the calculator gives it no power. Text prints in the printer's own font, 24 characters to a line as on the HP 82240B. A line with a character the printer lacks (√, Σ, π, the arrows, ×, ≠, ▸ and others) prints instead as a picture of the line in Plus42's own shapes, the same size as the printer's font. When the printing stops, the paper is fed up past the tear bar. Screen Print pulls down how PRLCD prints the screen here too: Portrait, the screen as it is, upright, dot for dot (it fits the paper); or Landscape, turned a quarter and three times bigger, filling the paper's width (turn the paper a quarter clockwise to read it).

    The USB port can be the computer's or the printer's, not both. So it's the computer's until something prints: then it becomes the printer's (about a second to find the printer the first time), and the computer's again 30 seconds after the last line. Meanwhile the computer doesn't see the calculator (no console, no drive). USB Drive → Connect to PC takes the port back at once. If no printer answers within 5 seconds, what was to print is dropped, and so is what prints in the next 10 seconds, so the calculator never waits for a printer that isn't there. A long listing waits for the printer with ▶ in the status bar; EXIT or R/S stops it, as with the HP 82240B. Choosing it also turns Plus42's printer on (PRON).

With a printer chosen, programs print too, and VIEW and AVIEW in a program print as well as show, as on an HP-42S with its printer on. The setting sets flags 21 and 55 at every start, so a PROFF or CF 21 lasts until the calculator is next turned on.

Apps print to the same printer, the one chosen here. PX-BASIC, for example, has LPRINT, LLIST and PRSCR.

States

A state is a complete copy of the calculator's memory (stack, variables, programs, settings) in a file. States are kept on the drive in /STATE as <name>.p42 files, the same format the desktop Plus42 uses.

States lists the saved states. The tabs act on the one picked; with none saved, only SAVE and EXIT show.

Programs can be kept on the drive as .raw files, in /PROGRAMS. This is the program file format of Plus42 and Free42 on computers and phones, and of the program libraries online. A .raw file holds one or more programs; it doesn't hold variables or the stack (use States for those).

9. Charging

The calculator runs on a built-in 1100 mAh lithium-ion battery, charged through the USB-C port from any USB charger or computer.

Charge LEDMeaning
RedCharging.
GreenCharged: the battery is full.
  • The calculator can be used while it charges.
  • The battery gauge at the right of the status bar shows the charge in four bars. Info in the settings menu shows the battery voltage: about 4.2 V when full.
  • When the battery is nearly empty, about 3.45 V, the calculator turns itself off to protect it. Its memory is kept while some charge remains, so charge it soon, then turn it on again. Left empty for long, the battery can run out completely, and the memory and the clock's time are lost: save important work as a state.
  • Turned off, the calculator uses very little power, about 135 µA, or 200 µA while the splash picture is showing. A full charge lasts months turned off, and many hours of use.
  • While a computer is connected over USB, the Sleep Timer doesn't turn the calculator off.

10. Updating the firmware

New firmware comes as a single .uf2 file. Installing it takes a USB-C cable and a computer (Windows, macOS or Linux); no software is needed. Saved states, program files, splash images, fonts, sounds, games and apps and the settings are on the calculator's drive, which an update doesn't touch.

From the settings menu

  1. Connect the calculator to the computer with the USB-C cable.
  2. In the settings menu, open USB Drive → Update Firmware and press ENTER. The new firmware starts with the memory cleared, as after any update: save the memory first if you want it back (Settings → States → Save State). Then a new drive named RP2350 appears on the computer: that's the update mode. The screen shows what to do, and the keys do nothing meanwhile.
  3. Drag the .uf2 file onto the RP2350 drive.
  4. Wait. When the copy finishes, the drive disappears by itself and the calculator starts the new firmware, with the memory cleared and the time as it was. The computer may warn that the drive was removed without ejecting it; that's normal here.

With the BOOT and RESET buttons

If the calculator doesn't respond, or to install firmware older than the Update Firmware item. This way clears the memory and may lose the time: save the memory first if you can (Settings → States → Save State).

  1. Connect the calculator to the computer with the USB-C cable.
  2. Press and hold the BOOT button.
  3. While holding BOOT, press and release the RESET button.
  4. Release BOOT. The RP2350 drive appears.
  5. Drag the .uf2 file onto it, and wait for the calculator to start.
  6. If you saved your memory, load it back: Settings → States → Load State. Set the time in Date & Time.

To check the update, Info in the settings menu shows the firmware version and the date it was built. To leave update mode without installing anything, press RESET. Like any RESET, it clears the calculator's memory.

Stopwatch

The stopwatch works like the one in the HP-41CX. It runs until you stop it, whatever else the calculator is doing, also while it is off. Setting the clock doesn’t change it. Memory Clear resets it, and so does a crash that restarts the calculator, to the time it had at the last OFF.

SW shows the time on the top line and gives the keyboard to the stopwatch until you press EXIT. Leaving doesn’t stop it. In a program, SW waits there, and the program goes on after EXIT.

12. Plus42 on your computer

Plus42 also runs on Windows, macOS and Linux, free from thomasokken.com/plus42. These skins make it look like the PX-42F, with the same keys and labels. You can write and try a program on the computer, then move it to the calculator as a .raw file (Settings → Programs). The screen inside the skin is Plus42's own, not the PX-42F's fonts and status bar.

A skin is two files with the same name: a .gif (the picture) and a .layout (where the keys and the display are). Plus42 needs both.

  1. Download both files of the skin.
  2. Put them in Plus42's folder:
    • macOS: ~/Library/Application Support/Plus42 (in the Finder, Go → Go to Folder, and paste it).
    • Windows: %APPDATA%\Plus42 (paste it into the File Explorer's address bar), or the folder that holds Plus42.exe.
    • Linux: ~/.local/share/plus42.
  3. Quit Plus42 and open it again, then choose PX42F from the Skin menu.
